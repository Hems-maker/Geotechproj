What is the Falling Head Test?

The Falling Head Test is a laboratory procedure used to
determine the coefficient of permeability of fine-grained
soils such as silts and clays. The test measures the rate
at which water flows through a soil sample under a
changing hydraulic head.

This website helps users perform computations instantly
while also understanding the theory, formulas, and
procedures involved in the test.

Importance of the Falling Head Test

The Falling Head Test is essential in geotechnical
engineering because it helps determine the drainage
characteristics of soil. Understanding soil
permeability is important in:

  • Foundation design
  • Seepage analysis
  • Earth dam engineering
  • Groundwater studies
  • Soil drainage evaluation

LABORATORY PROCEDURE:

  1. Initial State: Water is filled up to a designated high level, marked as the initial head (H1).
  2. The Flow: The valve at the bottom is opened, letting water percolate through the soil sample.
    Because the soil is fine-grained, the water level drops slowly.
  3. Final State: After a specific elapsed time interval (t), the water level drops to a lower mark,
    recorded as the final head (H2).

Overview

This website was created to assist civil engineering students, educators, and professionals in performing Falling Head Test computations accurately and efficiently.

Our goal is to simplify the computation process while helping users understand the principles behind soil permeability and hydraulic conductivity.
